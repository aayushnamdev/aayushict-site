The Speech That Said Nothing but Moved Everything

Friday's Jackson Hole speech, the trade I took, and why I closed it early

Aayush Namdev8 min read
Speech starts→Price drops→Two opposite lines→Long, closed at 38→Market reprices September

7:30 PM, two screens

Friday. One Chrome tab playing the speech from Jackson Hole. Nasdaq on the one minute chart next to it.

The second he started talking, price dropped.

Nothing had been said yet. He was still doing the opening.

That is the first thing worth noticing. Nobody was reacting to information. Everybody was reacting to the fact that information was coming.

Then he said two opposite things

He said the economy is doing fine. Consumer spending healthy. Labour market stable, not something he is worried about.

If you have been trading the last three weeks, that sounds like good news. The whole reason the market stopped expecting a rate hike was the jobs report on August 7 that came at minus 23,000.

But then he said inflation has not meaningfully improved. That this summer's better numbers do not tell him the underlying trend has changed.

So which one is it.

I could not tell you

I sat there and I did not know where he was tilting.

I want to be honest about that, because people are going to write about Friday like the message was obvious. It was not. I was watching it live, in a market I trade every day, and my read was 50/50.

What took the market an hour to work out

Those two statements are not opposites. They combine.

A stable labour market is exactly what removes the excuse not to raise rates.

When jobs are collapsing, the Fed has a reason to sit still even with inflation high. When jobs are fine and inflation is still stuck above 3, there is no reason left to wait.

He never said the word September. He did not have to.

I had the area marked two days earlier

A on the daily chart. An area where price moved so fast it left a gap behind, and price tends to come back to it.

I had no idea what would happen when it got there. I only knew it wanted to go there.

These are two different things

Where price goes is something I can plan.

What happens when it arrives is something I have to watch.

People mix these up constantly.

The trade

The initial drop finished. Price turned up. I took it long.

Nasdaq ran into the area and I closed at 38 handles.

Small. Nothing special. But why I closed there is the point of this whole article.

[Chart: NQ 1 minute, long into the daily FVG, closed at 38 handles]Tap to enlarge

Reason one, the rule

Price had already come up around 150 handles off the low.

By the time I was in profit we were in the expensive part of that range, not the cheap part. That is a rule. My framework tells me that and I do not argue with it.

Reason two, the one that mattered

I could not read the speech.

If I cannot tell you which way the man is tilting, it is a coin flip. You do not size up on a coin flip.

Price could have run to the Asian high. It could have sat there doing nothing for three hours. Both were live.

So I took the money and I got up.

Then I came back

About an hour later.

Nasdaq had dumped. Not drifted. Dumped. Gold was down around 3% on the day, 4,640 to 4,474.

Everything that went up went down harder.

One thing I should sayI watch gold on the daily chart, not intraday. Same with the dollar. So for Friday all I have on gold is where it started and where it ended. I did not see the shape of the fall. That is why this article is detailed on Nasdaq and not on gold.

Looking at it now

That push into the gap was manipulation. In ICT terms, a . Price goes up, takes liquidity from everyone who bought the bounce, then goes where it was always going.

But I am telling you that on Saturday, with the candle finished on my screen.

I could not have told you that at 8 PM on Friday. Nobody could.

Anyone showing you that chart today and saying it was obvious is showing you a chart that already ended.

What happened in that hour

The market changed its mind about September.

On Thursday, the odds of a rate hike at the September meeting were 35%. By Friday afternoon they were 61%. That is on CME FedWatch, and you can go look at it yourself.

The two year yield hit its highest level in a month. The dollar index went up to around 99.50.

The whole market moved to the other side in one session.

Now here is the part I want you to sit with

He gave no guidance.

He did not say September. He did not say what number would make him raise rates. He did not give any condition at all.

And the market still repriced that meeting by 26 points in a day.

Think about what that means. The market was not reacting to information, because there was no new information about September in that speech.

The market was guessing.

This is not an accident

Warsh has been doing this since he took the job in May.

He has cut back on forward guidance. He has made the Fed statement much shorter. He has given vague answers at both of his press conferences.

At Jackson Hole he said forward guidance has overstayed its welcome. He said markets should not be looking to the Fed for their next trade.

He is not being unclear by mistake. He thinks the Fed telling you the answer in advance is the problem, because then everyone trades the Fed instead of trading the data.

Why that matters for you

For more than twenty years, the Fed chair used this same Jackson Hole stage to tell the market which way rates were going. Last year Powell hinted at cuts from that stage and set off a rally.

The pattern was, the Fed tells you first, then the data comes and confirms it.

That is over.

Now the data comes first and you find out what it meant on the day it lands.

Every jobs number and every inflation number from here has to do the work that guidance used to do for it.

How this actually shows up in my trading

I trade Nasdaq. Mostly scalping. And I have two modes.

About 90% of the time, I cannot tell you the direction. I can make the argument for both sides. On those days I take my 1 to 1 and I go home. That is what Friday was.

About 10% of the time, it is clear. The August 7 jobs report was one of those. The number came at minus 23,000 against 85,000 expected, which was a huge miss, and from my scenarios I already knew what a miss like that meant for Nasdaq. I held that one for 102 handles and it was smooth the whole way.

The difference between 38 handles and 102 handles was not my chart reading. It was whether the fundamental picture was readable.

So what changes

Less guidance means fewer readable days.

Fewer readable days means more days in the 90% mode and fewer in the 10% mode.

That is not a prediction about the economy. That is just what happens to a trader when the input gets less clear.

And if you are newer to this, please hear the useful part of that. Getting smaller when you cannot read the situation is not being scared. It is the skill.

What I expect from here

Before a big number, the market goes quiet. It prices in the forecast over the days before, then Thursday chops, the London session chops, everybody is waiting.

Then the number drops and it gets violent.

That has always been true. I am not telling you that is new.

What I expect is that the violent part gets bigger. Because there is no guidance cushioning the surprise anymore, the surprise has to be absorbed entirely by price.

I want to be clear that this is my expectation, not a proven fact. Analysts who study this say the same thing, that price discovery moves onto the market when the committee says less. But nobody has enough data yet to confirm it. Ask me again in six months.

Friday, September 4, 6 PM India time. The August jobs report.

A bad number doesn't always move price. Here is why, using one real Friday night.

Aayush NamdevUpdated 8 Aug 20266 min read

On Friday 7 August, the jobs number came in at −23K. The forecast was 85K. That is a big miss.

Most people expect a big miss to cause a big move. It did move. But less than you would think, because a lot was already .

How to read the next release

  1. Check the forecast and the previous number.
  2. Look at what moved in the week before.
  3. Ask how much room is left to run.
The news is not the trade. The surprise is the trade.
Go deeper: why the dollar fell

Unemployment held at 4.1%, but job growth turned negative. That pulls rate cuts closer. Cuts make the dollar less attractive, so it slid while gold and Nasdaq rose.
