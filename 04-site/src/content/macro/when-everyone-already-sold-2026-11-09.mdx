## The number that did nothing

On Friday, America's jobs report came out at 7 PM India time.

The forecast was 65,000 new jobs. The actual number was 165,000. Almost three times bigger.

Unemployment fell to 4.1%. Wages rose 0.4% in one month.

<ShotFrame src={shot01} mobile={phone01} alt="Forex Factory: non farm employment change 165K actual, 65K forecast" caption="Friday: 165K against 65K expected" id="shot-01" />

A strong jobs number means America's RBI, the Fed, has room to keep raising rates. Higher rates usually mean a stronger dollar and pressure on gold.

So gold should have fallen hard. It didn't.

In the first minute, gold dropped about 35 points. Then it went sideways for hours. Many people asked me why. So did I.

<ShotFrame src={shot05} mobile={phone05} alt="Gold futures 1 minute chart: a sharp drop at 8:30 ET, then sideways chop" caption="Gold, 1 minute: one drop, then chop" id="shot-05" tall />

## Priced in, explained

The market does not trade the news. It trades what the news changes about the Fed's next move.

So the real question on Friday was not "how many jobs?" It was "how much did this change the December decision?"

CME FedWatch shows what big traders are betting on. Before the number, about 73% already expected a December hike.

<ShotFrame src={shot02} mobile={phone02} alt="CME FedWatch before the jobs report: 73% probability of a December hike" caption="Before the number: 73% already expected a hike" id="shot-02" />

After 165,000 jobs, that went to about 76%.

<ShotFrame src={shot03} mobile={phone03} alt="CME FedWatch after the jobs report: 76.3% probability of a December hike" caption="After 165K: only 76%" id="shot-03" />

A huge surprise in the number. A tiny change in the decision. Three points.

<ShotFrame src={shot04} mobile={phone04} alt="Process card: jobs 165K, Fed can keep hiking, hike bets from 73% to 76%" caption="The chain, and why it stayed small" id="shot-04" />

Think of a cricket match. India is losing badly. Everyone is 73% sure it is over, so most fans have already left the stadium.

Then the wicket falls. A few more fans walk out. And then there is nobody left to walk out.

<ShotFrame src={shot06} mobile={phone06} alt="Illustration: a stadium with mostly empty seats and a few fans walking to the exit" caption="Empty seat: already sold. Filled seat: still waiting." id="shot-06" />

That is what Friday looked like. Most of the selling happened before 7 PM, while traders were placing bets on the hike. The last few sellers acted in the first minute. After that, there was no fuel for a bigger move.

<PullLine>The market does not move on the news. It moves on the surprise.</PullLine>

<MoreBox title="Why chasing the move after the number hurt">

If you sold gold after the first candle, you were selling to a crowd that had already sold.

Nobody was left to push price lower. So price chopped, and stops got hit on both sides.

This is why I never trade the number itself. I wait for the number, let the first whipsaw settle, and then check if there is still a move left.

</MoreBox>

One honest note. Nobody can fully prove why a market moved. On Friday, Goldman Sachs also cut its gold forecast before the data, and the dollar rose 0.7%. But the 73% to 76% change is the clearest piece of evidence.

## The opposite case: a move nobody priced

Now look at Nasdaq on Thursday.

The Democrats won both houses of Congress on Tuesday. Many voters are angry about rising electricity bills, and many blame AI data centres. Some of the winners want limits on new ones.

The market did not have that priced in. Nasdaq 100 fell 6.4% in one day, about 2.3 trillion dollars wiped out.

<ShotFrame src={shot07} mobile={phone07} alt="Nasdaq futures daily chart: a large crash candle on Thursday 5 November" caption="Nasdaq, daily: the move nobody priced in" id="shot-07" />

Same week. Two events. One was expected, so the move was small. One was a surprise, so the move was huge.

A quick look at the rest:
1. **The dollar** rose about 0.7% on Friday, to around 102.3, back above its recent box.
2. **Oil** stayed near 90. Little news, little move.
3. **US 10 year yields** rose slightly, to about 4.86%.

## How to use this for CPI week

The same lens works for every big number. Before the release, ask two questions:
1. What is the forecast?
2. How much of the outcome is already expected?

Tuesday, 10 November, 7:00 PM IST is CPI, America's price report. It tells you how fast prices rose last month.

<ShotFrame src={shot08} mobile={phone08} alt="Forex Factory: CPI forecast 0.3% vs 0.6% previous, core CPI forecast 0.2% vs 0.3%" caption="Tuesday: CPI 0.3% forecast, core 0.2%" id="shot-08" />

Right now about 76% already expect a December hike. That means there is more room on one side than the other.
