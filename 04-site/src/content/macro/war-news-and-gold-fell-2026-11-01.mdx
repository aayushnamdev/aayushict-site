## Everyone is predicting a crash

My feed is full of reels right now. A crash is coming. Save your money for the next 12 months.

Maybe. I don't know. I'm not a long term investor. I'm a day trader.

So my question is different. What is the market doing right now? And where is the money going?

On Friday, I did nothing. I wasn't reading the news. I wasn't trading. I was just waiting for the daily close.

On Saturday, I opened the charts. Honestly? Amazing. Nasdaq broke below the box. Now things are clear.

But one thing didn't make sense at first. There was war news on Friday. And gold fell.

War news, and gold fell. Why?

## The old rule

Here is the rule most of us learned.

When stocks fall, gold goes up. Money runs from risk to safety. Like a seesaw. One side goes down, the other side goes up.

Most weeks, it roughly works like that. This week, it didn't.

## This week, the seesaw broke

Wednesday, the Fed held rates at 3.75 to 4%. But the chairman, Kevin Warsh, did not sound relaxed. He said inflation remains elevated. On financial conditions, he said he was "unwilling and unable to call them tight."

In simple words, no promise of help.

Thursday, the data came in hot. Headline PCE, the inflation number the Fed watches most, rose 0.5% in one month. That is 3.6% for the year.

Friday, a tanker was attacked in the Strait of Hormuz. That is the narrow sea route a big share of the world's oil passes through.

War news. The old rule says gold goes up. Now look at what actually happened.

Gold fell about 3.3%. December gold futures settled at $3,898.90. A one year low.

## Where the money went

So where did it go? I think bonds.

Quick one first. A bond is a loan. You lend the US government $100. They pay you interest every year. The yield is that interest, in percent.

Right now, the 10 year US bond pays close to 5%. Friday it closed at 4.94%. In early October, it touched about 5.3%. That was the highest since 2002.

<ShotFrame src={shot07} mobile={phone07} alt="US 10 year yield near 5%" caption="The 10 year US bond: close to 5%" id="shot-07" />

Now think like a big fund.

A safe US government bond pays you almost 5% a year. For doing nothing. So why hold Nasdaq, with all this risk? Why hold Bitcoin? Why even hold gold, which pays you nothing at all?

<MoreBox title="Why yields fell on Friday, and why that matters">

This sounds backwards, so I'll go slow.

Say a bond pays a fixed $5 a year on $100. That is 5%.

Now lots of people want that bond. The price goes up, say to $102. You still get only $5. So your yield is now a bit below 5%.

So when people rush into bonds, the price goes up and the yield goes down.

On Friday, the 10 year yield went down a little. That means people were buying bonds. That is the fingerprint. Money left stocks, gold and Bitcoin, and some of it sat in bonds.

</MoreBox>

And the Fed is not coming to help yet. On CME FedWatch, about 71% still expect a hike in December. Earlier in the week, it was about 82%.

<ShotFrame src={shot08} mobile={phone08} alt="CME FedWatch: about 71% probability of a December hike" caption="December: about 71% still expect a hike" id="shot-08" tall />

So here is the picture. A safe bond pays almost 5%. And most people still expect the Fed to hike, not cut. Nobody is promising to save you.

That is why the money is sitting out.

## Why even gold got sold

This was the part that confused me first.

When people panic, they need cash. Maybe they lost big on Nasdaq. Maybe their broker wants more money to keep a trade open. That is called a margin call.

So what do they sell? Whatever is easy to sell. And whatever is in profit.

Gold has been the big winner. Look at the monthly chart. From 2024 it went up and up. It peaked near 5,500 in January.

<ShotFrame src={shot09} mobile={phone09} alt="Gold monthly chart: a big run up since 2024" caption="Gold monthly: big profits since 2024" id="shot-09" />

If you need cash fast, you sell the winner. You lock the profit. You don't sell the thing that is already down 30%.

This is not new. On 16 October, traders sold metals to cover losses in other places. Same thing.

And there is one more push. Oil. Oil up means prices up. Prices up means the Fed keeps rates high. High rates hurt gold, because gold pays no interest. The bond does.

So war news, and gold fell. Not because gold stopped being safe. Because people needed cash, and gold was the easiest thing to sell.

## My read on the next 12 months

Am I calling a crash? No. That's not me.

But I agree things look tough.

Rates are high. I think they stay high for a long time. Oil is high. Companies are cutting costs to protect their share price. Last week, Microsoft offered voluntary buyouts to about 60,000 employees. Layoffs are still on the table.

That is a lot of fear. And fear is when people sell things and sit in cash.

So I'm not telling you to save everything for 12 months. I'm saying respect what the chart shows. Right now, it shows down.

## What could bring the money back

Three things I'm watching.

1. **The jobs report.** Friday 6 November. 8:30 AM New York, **7:00 PM India time.** US clocks changed today, so it is 7, not 6.
2. **Peace talks.** On Saturday, MRKT showed Iranian envoys arriving in Doha for talks. Talks are not a deal. But if they work, oil can fall.
3. **The Fed's tone.** Any sign that hikes are off the table.

<MoreBox title="Why a weak jobs report can be good news">

The Fed has two jobs. Keep prices stable. Keep people employed.

If jobs come in weak, the Fed has a reason not to hike. Fewer hikes mean lower yields. A bond paying less makes gold and stocks look better again.

So a bad number for workers can be a good number for markets. Strange, but that's how it works.

</MoreBox>
