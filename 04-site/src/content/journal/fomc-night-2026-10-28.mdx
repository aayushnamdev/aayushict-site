## I didn't come for the decision

The Fed decision was at 11:30 PM India time. 2:00 PM in New York.

I wasn't looking for a trade at 11:30. A hold was already about 90% priced in. Everyone knew the number. A known number doesn't move anything.

What I was waiting for was the press conference at midnight. That's when the chairman talks. And the chairman's words, not the number, are what decide the next few weeks.

I came in with no bias. Not bullish, not bearish. Just listening.

The decision came out as expected. Rates held at 3.75 to 4%. But the vote was 10 to 2. Two members wanted a hike right then.

---

## Then Warsh started talking

Warsh didn't sound relaxed. He said inflation remains elevated. He said financial conditions are tighter, but he is "unwilling and unable to call them tight." No promise about December. No relief either.

That was my read: a chairman who is still worried about inflation, with oil still high. That told me the risk was to the downside for Nasdaq.

So I decided I was only interested in a sell. Now I just needed price to show me.

## What price did

While he was speaking, price ran straight up into the 21 October daily gap at 29,984. It took the buyside liquidity above the highs and tagged around 30,009.

That's the kind of run I wait for. Price goes up to grab the stops, right into a level that matters.

<ShotFrame src={shot02} mobile={phone02} alt="NQ 1 hour: price runs into the 21 Oct daily gap and takes buyside liquidity" caption="NQ 1 hour: price runs into the 21 Oct daily gap and takes buyside liquidity" id="shot-02" />

Then, around 12:16 AM India time, this headline hit:

> Fed's Warsh says inflation compensation shows improvement as Fed removes accommodation in September; sees "information rich" period ahead.

In plain words: more data is coming, and he is watching all of it. Nothing that pointed to cuts. Price rolled over within minutes.

## The entry

I waited for price to displace below the iFVG, around 29,940 to 29,962. Once it did, I took the short there, with a proper stop.

<ShotFrame src={shot04} mobile={phone04} alt="NQ 1 minute: displacement below the iFVG and the drop (New York time)" caption="NQ 1 minute: displacement below the iFVG and the drop (New York time)" id="shot-04" />

Price dropped to around 29,840.

I took profit at about 1 to 1.2R.

Why so small? Because my bias didn't come from a chart. It came from the chairman's tone. And he was still talking. One sentence could have flipped everything. When the person moving the market still has the microphone, I don't get greedy.

---

## After the exit

I closed the chart.

That's it. I didn't watch what happened next. I didn't look for a second trade. One trade, done. I came back the next day.

For the record, NQ whipsawed across about 500 points that night. A lot of people got chopped up both ways.

---

## The lesson

The number at 11:30 was never the trade.

The trade came from listening to the chairman, then waiting for price to agree with what I heard.

And when the person moving the market is still talking, take what's there and leave.

---

## Where Nasdaq is now

On the daily chart, I don't like it. At all.

After that night, I expected price to pick a side. Either close above the box or close below it. It did neither. It's sitting right around the middle of the range, roughly 29,100 to 30,100, and has been chopping there for two weeks.

<ShotFrame src={shot05} mobile={phone05} alt="NQ daily: price chopping in the middle of the range" caption="NQ daily: price chopping in the middle of the range" id="shot-05" />

Inside a range like this, my win rate drops close to 50%. With my system, that's not profitable. So the best trade for me is no trade.

I'm stepping away from Nasdaq until price closes outside this box and shows real displacement. Then I'll come back.

The next real test is the jobs report on Friday, 6 November. Note the new time: **7 PM India time, not 6.** America changes its clocks on 1 November.
