# The Speech That Said Nothing but Moved Everything

SUBTITLE: I was watching it live and I still could not read it

## Friday, 7:30 PM

There was a tab inside Chrome which was playing the live speech, and I was watching the live market on the one minute, specifically Nasdaq.

It went down first, at the exact start of the speech.

I was not convinced about that, because there was nothing to be said yet. He had not said anything. But price was already moving down.

## Then he started, and it got confusing

He said the labour market is under control. It is not something he is worried about right now. He is in control of the Fed, and that is the area where I focused.

But on the other side he is talking about inflation, which is our concern. The 2% goal.

That was the confusing part.

The speech was not clear to me. He is telling about the labour market being okay and stable, and then he is telling about inflation not improving.

So which one is he tilting to. I could not tell you.

## What I could not see at the time

Those two things are not opposites. They add up.

A stable labour market is exactly what removes the excuse not to raise rates. When jobs are collapsing, the Fed has a reason to sit still even with inflation high. When jobs are fine and inflation is still stuck above 3, there is no reason left to wait.

The market took about an hour to work that out. So did I, and I was watching it live.

He never said the word September. He did not have to.

## The level I was watching

There was a daily fair value gap acting like a magnet for price to come towards. That is the narrative I was looking at.

I knew that almost two days back. That this is a good area where price can gravitate towards.

But I did not know what would happen there. I will judge based on the reaction from that level, and that is what I always do.

## The trade

There was a strong confirmation towards the upside, so I took my trade.

It was a very short scalp. Nothing crazy. 38 handles in NQ.

I had a feeling it would continue to go up, for the Asian high liquidity. But it had already moved 150 handles from the low, so I did not want to let it run.

## Why I closed there

Two reasons.

First, the speech. I could not find the exact tone, where he was tilting. If I cannot figure out, then it is a 50/50 probability on both sides. Maybe it could have just consolidated without going anywhere for a long time.

Second, the range. It had already come up almost 150 handles from the bottom, so my trade was in the premium area, not in the very low discount area relative to that range.

So I took it and I got up.

## Then I came back

After maybe one hour.

Price had already dumped. Strong dumping in Nasdaq. In gold everything just dumped, around 3% down, 4,640 to 4,474.

That is the thing that surprised me. It went down first, then it went up, and then it just crashed.

One thing I should say here. I do not look at gold from the intraday perspective. I look at the one hour when the moves are violent, but generally I look at it from the daily chart. Same with DXY. So for Friday all I have on gold is where it started and where it ended.

## Looking at it now

That move up was manipulation. The Judas, what we call it in ICT terminology.

But I am saying that now, on Saturday, with the candle finished on my screen.

The move towards the upside was pure predictable, and that is where I participated. The falling down leg was confusing. It was not something that could be predicted from before.

That is the difference I want you to understand. Where price goes is something I can plan. What happens when it arrives is something I have to watch.

Anyone showing you that chart today and telling you it was obvious is showing you a chart that already ended.

## What happened during that hour

The market changed its mind about September.

On Thursday, the odds of a rate hike at the September meeting were 35%. By Friday afternoon they were 61%. That is CME FedWatch, you can check it yourself.

The two year yield went to its highest in a month. The dollar index went up to around 99.50.

## And here is the part

He gave no guidance.

He did not say September. He did not say what number would make him raise rates. No condition at all.

And the market still moved that meeting by 26 points in one day.

So the market was not reacting to information about September, because there was none in that speech.

The market was guessing.

## He is doing this on purpose

Warsh has been like this since he took the job in May. Less forward guidance. Much shorter Fed statement. Vague answers at both of his press conferences.

At Jackson Hole he said forward guidance has overstayed its welcome. He said markets should not be looking to the Fed for their next trade.

For more than twenty years the Fed chair used that same stage to tell the market which way rates were going. Last year Powell hinted at cuts from there and set off a rally.

The pattern was, Fed tells you first, then the data confirms it.

That is over. Now the data comes first and you find out on the day.

## How this shows up in my own trading

I am a scalper. The majority of my trades, I will say 90%, look the same.

Even on a normal day the market opens, the NY session starts, and I am not sure about the direction. It is not clear, it is not one sided, I can make the argument for both sides. Then I just focus on my basic 1 to 1 risk to reward, and that is how I trade.

Friday was that.

Then there is the other 10%. Last NFP, the number came minus and it was way off from the forecast. Based on my scenarios I already wanted Nasdaq to go up, because that was predetermined. If the number comes off from the forecast then there is a very high chance it goes that way, and that is a probability in one sidedness.

That is where I captured a 105 handle move, and it was amazing. It was very smooth.

The difference between 38 handles and 105 handles was not my chart reading. It was whether the fundamental side was readable or not.

## So what changes now

The whole focus goes towards news specific things. NFP, inflation, all these data points.

What will happen is the market will start to guess what is going to happen in NFP, based on the forecast. It will align towards that and it will price in those things. So on the day itself price will not move much, maybe in the past one or two days it already priced the number in.

And if something surprising happens against the forecast, then there will be violent moves. Big swings, big direction. Very clear direction. That is the time when the 10% gets unlocked.

On the majority of days when I am not clear with direction, I will stick with the 90%. Just scalping.

## This part is not new

I have seen this many times over the past few years.

The market was expecting something based on the forecast, so it already moved before the number. Let us say from Monday to Wednesday it already moved for a good three days. Then Thursday before NFP it just chops in big ranges. It consolidates. Even the London session before NFP goes in chopping mode, because the market is in waiting mode.

Once the number drops, things get violent. You see huge moves in price swings, because the market was waiting for a number and it happened.

So I am not telling you this is new.

What I expect is that the violent part gets bigger, because there is no guidance cushioning the surprise anymore. The surprise has to be absorbed fully by price.

That is my expectation, not a proven fact. People who study this say the same thing, that price discovery moves onto the market when the committee says less. But nobody has enough data yet on this Fed. Ask me again in six months.

## The next test

Friday, September 4, 6 PM India time. The jobs report.

Then inflation data on September 11. Then the Fed decides on September 16.

Right now that meeting is a coin flip. Nobody has an edge at 50/50.

So watch that Friday. If a speech with no guidance in it moved the meeting by 26 points, think about what an actual number does.

I will be at the screen. Probably in 90% mode.

Not financial advice. Trade with a stop loss.
